Idea: at city-wide zoom the map shows only the choropleth. Stations, pins, clusters, rings — all gated until you zoom in. Chrome shrinks to a summary card + one FAB that opens a bottom sheet with filters & lens controls.

  • Power users lose the always-visible chip-state cues.

Idea: drop the choropleth entirely (or keep it as a near-invisible backdrop). Clusters carry the visualization at every zoom. Numbers and density are immediate. Click a cluster → zooms into it. Filter chrome stays roughly as today.

  • Density is visible without a scale legend — counts tell the story.
  • Fixes the "no clusters appear" bug naturally.
  • Closer to the dominant Mapbox/Carto pattern users expect.
  • Loses the editorial choropleth aesthetic that differentiates the site.
  • Doesn't address the transit-lens chaos directly.

Idea: the left rail becomes the primary surface. Lens picker, filters, and the browse list all live there. Map stays clean — only what's selected gets annotated. Selecting a neighborhood row pans & zooms the map; selecting a transit station shows its shed.

  • Solves the lens-overlap problem: the map only ever has the active selection.
  • Scannable data list is always present — no need to expand a panel.
  • Resembles the "magazine reading view" spirit of the brand.
  • Loses ~25% of horizontal map area.
  • Mobile collapse requires care — the sidebar becomes a bottom sheet.
  • Click-on-map workflow becomes secondary.

Idea: the lens never adds a new layer — it recolors existing pins. "Zoning lens" tints each permit by the district it sits in. "Transit lens" tints by distance band. No station markers cluttering the view; no concentric rings.

  • Solves transit chaos completely: no station markers needed.
  • One thing on the map (pins) tells you three things via color.
  • Filter chips and lens picker share one toolbar.
  • Stations as discrete entities go away — no per-station shed filter.
  • Color encoding overloads readers who don't want to interpret legends.
  • Needs cluster-color blending logic (heterogeneous clusters).
