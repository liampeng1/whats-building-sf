What's Building SF — alternatives to "Browse all" popup

Four alternatives that keep the data discoverable without a separate popup. Each sits inside Direction A's chrome. The list shown is "all 41 neighborhoods sorted by permit count" in Neighborhoods lens; the same surface morphs to zoning cats / transit bands when the lens switches.

Idea: the top-right summary card grows into a top-N list when you tap it. Default state shows top 5 inline as a quick-glance leaderboard. "See all 41" expands the card to scroll the full list. Click any row to drill in. No separate popup ever appears.

  • Browse data is always visible — the top-N is part of the chrome.
  • Discoverable without a button hunt.
  • One surface owns "summary + list" — less UI to learn.
  • Card grows tall, can cover the upper-right corner of the map.
  • Always-on data means users can't fully clear the chrome.

Idea: the lens rail is the entry point for browsing. Tap the active pill again → the rail grows upward into a scrollable list of items in that lens. The mode switcher stays inline at the top. Closes with X or by tapping the active pill a third time.

  • Discovery is in the place users already touch to change lens.
  • Compact — doesn't fight for top-right real estate.
  • Lens + browse share one container, conceptually correct.
  • "Tap-twice" interaction is unusual; needs a UI affordance hint.
  • Mobile thumb reach: bottom-left list pushes upward toward map content.

Idea: a slim 200px sidebar permanently anchored to the left edge. Shows the full list, scrolls naturally. Collapses to a thin handle for users who want pure-map view. The lens rail in the strip is the canonical mode switcher; the bottom-left rail still works as a secondary.

  • No "popup" anywhere — most explicit, least hidden state.
  • Persistent list rewards scanning; great for repeat visitors.
  • Mobile collapses gracefully to bottom sheet on narrow viewport.
  • Map loses ~15% horizontal real estate by default.
  • Compromises Direction A's "chrome recedes" intent.
  • Two lens-pill rows visible (sidebar + bottom-left) until one is hidden.

Idea: the data list is replaced entirely by direct map interaction. Hover any neighborhood/zoning polygon → floating tip with name + permit count + rank. Click → the panel for that thing. No leaderboard surface anywhere; the map IS the index.

  • The cleanest map view of all four options.
  • Encourages spatial exploration (what's around X) over alphabetic.
  • No new chrome — reuses existing polygon click behavior.
  • Loses scannable "top-N by permit count" entirely.
  • Hover-only on desktop; mobile needs a long-press fallback.
  • Hard to discover the #1 neighborhood without already looking at the right area.
