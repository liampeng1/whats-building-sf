What's Building SF — Station / transit-marker redesign, four directions

All four sit inside Direction A's chrome (summary top-right, FAB bottom-right, lens rail bottom-left). Each shows the city-wide transit lens at zoom ~13. Goal: make "where is transit" visible without drowning the map.

S1 · Tiered prominence major modes louder · bus stops zoomed-only

Idea: rank modes by significance. BART = anchor, large filled circle with center ring. Muni Metro = medium sage dot. Caltrain = gold square. Rapid bus stops gated to zoom ≥ 14 (close-up only). Subway stations always visible; surface light-rail and bus appear progressively.

  • Reads in a glance — BART backbone immediately obvious.
  • ~18 markers at city zoom instead of 387.
  • Color-coded by mode without a separate legend lookup.
  • Bus shed advocacy lost at low zoom — users may not realize bus filter exists.
  • Three mode shapes & colors to remember.

Idea: render rail and rapid bus as lines, not points. A user looking at the map sees Market St corridor as a single line, not eight dots. Stations exist as faint tick marks on the lines, with only major BART/Caltrain station labels at city zoom. Click a line → list of permits along that corridor.

  • "Transit lens" tells the actual story: the city has rails and rapid corridors.
  • Zero marker clutter — line geometries are cheap visually.
  • Naturally supports a new filter: "permits along the 38R".
  • Per-station shed concept gets reframed as per-corridor.
  • Needs line geometries (GTFS shapes.txt) — we don't have them yet.
  • Visual style shifts: lines compete with road network on the basemap.

Idea: at city zoom, the transit lens is just two soft-tinted areas: navy = within ½ mi of rail, sienna = within 0.1 mi of rapid bus. No markers at all. Stations and stops appear as small dots only when zoomed past z 14. Answers the question "is this parcel transit-rich?" instantly, by color.

  • Most editorial of the four — clean, readable, magazine-like.
  • Conveys shed coverage at a glance, which is the real point of the lens.
  • Per-station shed filter still works once zoomed in.
  • Stations are invisible at the level most users will spend time at.
  • Two overlapping tinted areas can mush together in the central core.

Idea: count rail + rapid-bus stops per ½-mile cell, render as a continuous heatmap. Warmest cells = most transit. Just a handful of labels at the hottest hubs. Stations appear only at zoom-in, like S3, but the heatmap replaces the binary shed tint with a quantitative gradient.

  • Most information per pixel — shows transit *intensity* not just presence.
  • One color, one variable to read.
  • Pairs naturally with permit density (also a heatmap).
  • Heatmaps need careful tuning — bad bandwidth choice = blob soup.
  • "Transit shed" (½ mi binary) is what users learn — heatmap is a different vocabulary.
  • Per-station filter still requires a different interaction.
